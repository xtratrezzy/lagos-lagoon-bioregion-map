The Watershed 01 · Boundaries

Lagos Lagoon and its connected Lekki Lagoon sit within the coastal Dahomey sedimentary basin of southwest Nigeria, fed principally by the Ogun and Osun rivers draining down from the interior. The lagoon system empties into the Atlantic through two main outlets — the Lagos Harbour channel to the west and the Lekki-Epe channel to the east — forming one continuous estuarine watershed even though it's often treated administratively as two separate water bodies. Around 20 million people live within this catchment today.

Key Ecosystems 02 · Habitat

The lagoon's shoreline is fringed by mangrove swamp forest — part of a coastal mangrove belt that, at the national scale, makes Nigeria home to Africa's largest mangrove ecosystem. These fringing mangroves and the brackish wetlands behind them filter runoff, buffer the shoreline from erosion, and serve as a nursery habitat for the fish stocks much of the surrounding population depends on. The Lekki Peninsula also holds a protected pocket of freshwater swamp forest and rainforest, one of the last intact fragments of pre-urban vegetation left in the immediate metro area.

Environmental Pressures 03 · Field Notes

Industrial & Urban Pollution

Lagos Lagoon ranks among the most polluted African ecosystems by WHO/UN Environment data — heavy metals and hydrocarbon pollutants arrive via oil, textile, and urban sewage effluent carried down the Ogun and Osun rivers.

Mangrove Loss to Dredging

Large-scale sand dredging for land reclamation — building new coastal real estate developments — is actively burying and destroying mangrove forest along the lagoon's edges.

Flood Vulnerability

Lagos is ranked among the 50 cities most vulnerable to extreme sea level rise globally; roughly 12% of the state already floods seasonally, a share only rising with wetland loss.

Population Pressure

With 20+ million people concentrated around the lagoon, ongoing urban expansion continues to convert wetland buffer zones into hard, impermeable development.

Local Coordination 04 · Who's Working On This

Existing project · est. 1990

Nigerian Conservation Foundation — Lekki Conservation Centre

Nigeria's oldest conservation NGO, founded 1980, established and still runs the Lekki Conservation Centre directly within this bioregion — a protected wetland and forest reserve on the Lekki Peninsula. NCF's broader work includes wetland conservation programs and a national forest-recovery initiative aimed at rebuilding Nigeria's forest cover. It's one of the few standing, on-the-ground coordination points actually working inside this specific watershed, rather than studying it from outside.
